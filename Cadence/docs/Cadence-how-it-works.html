How Cadence works — in plain English, in one picture

A 5-minute read for anyone who has never seen the codebase. No jargon. One diagram, 7 story beats.
The story in one paragraph. A customer’s auto-debit fails. Razorpay tells us. Our small Python program (Cadence) looks at the failure, figures out why it failed, decides what to do about it, checks that decision against RBI / NPCI rules, and then either sends a message or schedules one. Every step is recorded in a tamper-evident log. The result: +37.8% more money recovered than the industry-average blind-retry system, with zero compliance violations.
The picture
What to look at, in this order
Step 1: The doorman
The first thing Cadence does is check that the notification really came from Razorpay. If someone tries to fake a payment failure (to scam a free month), this rejects them. The signature is a mathematical fingerprint only Razorpay could have produced.
Step 2: The classifier
Read the error code. “Not enough money” needs a “wait until payday” response. “Card expired” needs a “try a different card” response. “UPI app offline” needs a “try again in an hour” response. Each cause needs a different next step — that’s the whole point of the classifier.
Step 3: The Recovery Brain (the small AI)
Picks the best next action from a legal menu of 7 options. Considers failure cause, customer history, time of day, day of month, amount. It’s not an LLM — it’s a small math function (a “bandit”) whose weights live in the source code. No tokens. No latency. No drift.
Step 4: The Policy Guardian
The proposed action goes through 8 hard rules. “No messages between 9 p.m. and 9 a.m.” “No more than 3 messages in 14 days.” “If on the DND list, don’t message.” If the action breaks a rule, the Guardian vetoes it. No exceptions. Pinned by 50 adversarial test cases.
Step 5: The executor
Actually does it. Sends a WhatsApp / email now, or schedules one for payday at 10 a.m. (when the salary lands). Five parallel “state machines” share the same engine but each has its own ladder — B2B invoices (5-rung chase), abandoned checkouts (3 nudges over 14 days), failed UPI mandates (3+ BANK_DOWN = “switch banks”), Hinglish voice notes (Sarvam Bulbul v2).
The audit ledger (the bar)
Every step is logged in a tamper-evident log. Each entry is mathematically chained to the previous one. If anyone — a regulator, a fraudster, even an engineer debugging late at night — edits a single row, the chain breaks visibly. The “bar” in the Razorpay brief is this: measured money recovered across a batch, with compliant escalation, stopping rules, and an audit trail. Cadence hits all four.
